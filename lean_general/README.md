# Compact-range Vietoris powers of countable ordinals

Author: Lucas Alves de Almeida (lucasdeirva8x@gmail.com).

This project accompanies *Compact-Range Vietoris Powers of Countable Ordinals*. The recorded verification status, exact source hashes and audited declarations are in `verification-status.json`; compiler output is in `logs/` and `verification-audit.log`.

## Exact theorem and topology

`VietorisOrdinals.ordinal_main` states that for every actual mathlib ordinal `a : Ordinal.{0}` with `omega0 < a` and `a < omega 1`, the compact-range Vietoris power of the subtype `Set.Iio a` is second-countable, Lindelof, and not sigma-compact.

`VietorisOrdinals.ordinal_full` additionally states failure of the Menger property. `Menger` is defined using arbitrary open covers, finite selections from each cover, and the requirement that all selections together cover the space. It is not restricted to countable covers. The proof provides countable open covers that already defeat every finite selection.

The only mathematical hypotheses in the principal theorem are the two ordinal inequalities. The general helper lemmas have explicit structural hypotheses, all discharged in the ordinal specialization. No compactness conclusion, countability conclusion, occurrence bound or diagonal avoidance property is assumed in the final ordinal theorem.

`K X` is the subtype of maps `Nat -> X` whose actual ranges are compact. The topology is explicitly induced from the topology generated by Caruvana and Holshouser's basic sets: one open bound on the whole range and finitely many open coordinate restrictions. This is not an implicit use of the ordinary product topology. The ordinal ground topology is `Preorder.topology Ordinal`, and `Set.Iio a` inherits the subspace topology. Its order topology is checked by the audit.

The general countable-base theorem applies to every second-countable ground space, with countable index. The obstruction uses an injective enumeration of a compact copy of the convergent sequence whose finite points are isolated in the ambient space. The final theorem constructs this enumeration inside the actual ordinal interval and proves the required properties.

## Source correspondence and limits

The reference is Christopher Caruvana and Jared Holshouser, *An Adaptation of the Vietoris Topology for Ordered Compact Sets*, arXiv:2507.17936v3, Definitions 1.8 and 1.14 (pp. 4-5), and Question 1 (p. 11): https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17936v3 .

For a countable ordinal, every compact subset has cardinality at most aleph zero, and the source's convention adds aleph zero to the supremum of those cardinalities. Therefore the default index is countable. `ordinal_subsets_countable` checks the cardinal bound for every subset. The formal carrier uses `Nat` directly; the source's cardinal-supremum indexing expression is not itself the definition of `K` in this project. This interpretation is documented, not concealed as an extra axiom.

The delayed enumeration is adapted from the author's earlier manuscript *On the Vietoris Power of a Convergent Sequence*. `Diagonal.lean` preserves the earlier combinatorial proof and namespace. The new topological and ordinal results are in the `VietorisOrdinals` namespace.

The manuscript's closed-subspace transfer proposition is proved mathematically but is not a separate formal result in this package. The uncountable-ordinal case is attributed to Proposition 2.17 of the reference and is not included in the new formalization. These omissions do not affect the fully formalized principal theorem for the stated interval.

## Modules

- `Model.lean`: compact-range carrier and source topology; open tubes and continuous evaluations.
- `Diagonal.lean`: delayed enumeration and its exact occurrence bound.
- `Countability.lean`: finite-union refinement, countable generators, second countability and Lindelofness.
- `Obstruction.lean`: closed surjection family, compact occurrence bounds, non-sigma-compactness.
- `Menger.lean`: the standard selection property, bad open covers and non-Menger theorem.
- `Ordinals.lean`: actual ordinal topology, countability of the interval, compact initial segment, isolated finite points and the principal theorem.
- `Results.lean`: the full ordinal theorem and the cardinal bound for subsets.
- `FullAudit.lean`: explicit theorem-type checks, definition displays and transitive axiom reports.

## Reproduction

Pinned compiler: Lean 4.19.0 (commit 6caaee842e94).

Pinned mathlib: v4.19.0, commit c44e0c8ee63ca166450922a373c7409c5d26b00b. Preserve `lake-manifest.json` to retain all dependency revisions.

After installing Lean through elan, initialize dependencies from the project directory:

```text
lake exe cache get
```

Then on Windows run:

```powershell
.\verify.ps1
```

The script compiles each module, checks `FullAudit.lean`, requires all six requested axiom reports, and rejects any reported axiom outside `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

On other systems the corresponding commands are `lake build` followed by `lake env lean FullAudit.lean`; inspect all printed axiom lists and the process exit code. In particular, absence of error text alone is not an axiom audit.

The local run recorded in this package used the same pinned compiler directly, with `LEAN_PATH` pointing to the project's output library and the already-cached dependency libraries. Individual invocation logs and exit codes are retained. The archive omits installed toolchains, dependency repositories, and compiled `.olean` files; a fresh installation needs network access to obtain dependencies.

## Integrity and trust

`source-manifest.json` records SHA-256 hashes of the supplied proof sources, build metadata, reproduction script, and README. `verification-status.json` records the manifest hash and audit-log hash. These hashes identify files; only the actual successful Lean check and its transitive axiom audit substantiate verification.

The audit checks the principal theorem against an explicit conjunction rather than relying only on the name `MainClaim`. It also checks the order-topology instance and the explicit non-Menger conclusion. The standard classical foundations `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` are allowed and disclosed. Admitted proofs, custom mathematical axioms and native-computation trust axioms are not permitted in the reported final declarations.

This is a reproducible local verification record, not an institutional certificate, independent peer review, or a claim of publication priority.
